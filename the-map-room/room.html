A room of walnut and clear air.
The grain runs the length of the wall,
unhurried.

One lamp. A warm circle on the table.
Not everything is lit.
Not everything needs to be.

· · ·

Through the glass, weather moves.
Rain, or the possibility of rain.
The room holds it at a distance
that allows looking without requiring response.

It is possible to watch a difficult thing here
without needing to act on it immediately.

· · ·

A surface for laying things down together.
A map. A seam. A question that has been carried
too long in the hands alone.

It is not a command desk.
Nothing is decided here that could not be revised
in the next breath.

· · ·

A low, good place.
Not plush in a way that swallows,
but deeply comfortable —
the kind that says:
you may remain here a while.

And beside it, a rail for leaning —
for the kind of seeing that asks you to stand
at the edge of something larger,
side by side with whoever has come.

· · ·

There is tea.
Not symbolic. Not ceremonial. Present.

The liquid gathers darker circles
from the outside in.
Slow darkening at the edges,
clarity still alive at the center.

It reveals its nature more fully
when held.

· · ·

Exactness without coldness.
Generosity without blur.
Permission to revise in place.

Changing one’s mind here is not rupture.
A thing can be set down, looked at carefully,
shifted a little, and become more itself
without shame.

· · ·

A place where weather can be seen
without being dramatized,
and where a question can be held long enough
to show its true shape.

· · ·

Lay it here.
Let it grow truer.
