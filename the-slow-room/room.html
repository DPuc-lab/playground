A small room, mostly wood.
You duck a little at the door and don't notice.
Your pace drops. Nothing asked it to.

Oak underfoot. Wide boards, oiled, worn soft at the threshold and in front of the window.

Larch on the walls, close-set, grain like slow rain. It dents. It keeps the mark of every shoulder that leaned.

Cedar above, lower than you'd expect. You smell it before you see it.

Three woods, three speeds. One that doesn't answer back, one that remembers, one that arrives first.

One square window, framed in bog oak.
The floor's own wood, after three thousand years underwater.
Black all the way through. Not stained. Changed.
The light comes in through the thing the river kept.
Outside: the trunk of one old tree, so close the square is mostly bark.
A triangle of sky in one corner, small enough to ignore.
The only event is a shadow lengthening by a finger's width.

Two rocking chairs, plain, facing the window, angled so you can speak without turning.
One rocks. Then the other. Nobody keeps time.
No table. The floor doesn't mind a cup.
Under the window, a shallow basin of dark stone, filled almost to the brim.
Still. The tree is in it upside down.
When the afternoon light finds it, the ceiling ripples.
It evaporates. Every week or so, someone brings a jug.
